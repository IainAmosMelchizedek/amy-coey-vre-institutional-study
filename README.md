# While She Administered My Rehabilitation, I Studied the Politics of the Institution Through Her

## Amy Coey, VR&E, Political Authority, and a Four-Dimensional Study of Institutional Power

This repository preserves my relationship with **Amy Coey**, the Veterans Benefits Administration (VBA) Vocational Rehabilitation Counselor assigned to administer my participation in the **Veteran Readiness and Employment (VR&E)** program.

It is an archive of what occurred between **me and Amy Coey**: our correspondence, the rehabilitation plans she administered, the representations she made concerning my case, my objections, the governing records, the legal and policy authorities invoked, and my analysis of her conduct through the framework I formally articulated in 2019:

> **political, social, religious, and economic reasons**

Amy entered my life at an extraordinary political moment.

On **June 13, 2023**, agents of the Office of Inspector General searched my residence in connection with what I reject as a false federal data-breach narrative arising after my whistleblower activity at the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau.

On **June 14, 2023**, one day later, Amy interviewed me for VR&E.

During that interview, she asked whether I had a criminal history. I told her I did not. I also disclosed the OIG raid from the previous day and explained the whistleblower and federal-conflict context as I understood it.

Amy therefore did not meet me without context.

From the beginning of our relationship, she encountered me as a veteran attempting to rebuild my economic and professional life while simultaneously challenging the conduct of another federal institution.

This repository studies what happened next.

## This Is a Political Archive

The political dimension of this repository is not incidental.

It is one of its central subjects.

My position, developed contemporaneously and later stated at length in my affidavit, is that the conflict that emerged inside VR&E cannot be adequately understood as a series of ordinary administrative mistakes.

I came to interpret Amy's conduct as **ideological and political**.

More specifically, I assert that her later treatment of my case must be examined within the political geography and institutional relationships in which we were operating:

- my earlier service as a federal official in the Northeast;
- my whistleblower disclosures concerning the CFPB's Northeast Regional operations;
- my residence and VR&E case within the Massachusetts/Northeast federal environment;
- Senator **Elizabeth Warren's historic role in conceiving and helping establish the CFPB**;
- the political administration under which my VR&E relationship began;
- the government's contemporaneous characterization of me as an insider threat; and
- the institutional meaning that an official finding that I had been **"Rehabilitated"** would carry after those events.

My affidavit argues that these facts created a political contradiction.

I had been treated by parts of the federal government as a threat after challenging conduct at the CFPB.

At the same time, another federal component possessed the authority to conclude officially that I had successfully completed rehabilitation, achieved suitable employment, and satisfied the objectives of my approved plan.

From my perspective, those two narratives could not comfortably coexist.

That tension became central to my interpretation of Amy's conduct.

## The Political Meaning of "Rehabilitated"

The word **Rehabilitated** became more than an administrative status in my case.

In my affidavit, I argued that a formal finding of rehabilitation would have had consequences beyond VR&E.

It would have created an official federal record recognizing that I:

- completed the objectives established in my rehabilitation plan;
- completed graduate education in analytics;
- remained compliant with the healthcare component of the plan;
- participated in employment services;
- pursued lawful economic activity;
- achieved vocational progress; and
- satisfied the conditions I understood to govern successful rehabilitation.

I further argued that such a finding would stand in profound tension with the political and institutional narrative that had surrounded me since the CFPB conflict.

In my analysis, declaring me **Rehabilitated** would not merely close a benefits case.

It would recognize my legitimacy.

It would record successful reintegration.

It would place an official federal conclusion of rehabilitation beside an earlier federal narrative portraying me as dangerous, improper, or threatening.

That is why the closure dispute occupies such an important place in this archive.

## Law Versus Policy

One of the most important political questions in this repository is the distinction between **law** and **internal administrative policy**.

My objection was not merely that Amy reached a result I disliked.

My objection was that I repeatedly sought the **legal and regulatory authority** governing the result and concluded that the classification she attempted to apply to me did not arise from the governing regulation on which I relied.

On **July 14, 2025**, I formally objected to the proposed classification of my case under **Maximum Rehabilitation Gain (MRG)**.

I stated that MRG did not appear in the governing VR&E regulations in **38 C.F.R. Part 21**, and I requested citation to statutory or regulatory authority defining or authorizing that classification.

My position was that the governing legal question concerned whether I met the criteria for **Rehabilitated** status under **38 C.F.R. § 21.283**.

The next day, rather than resolving the objection to MRG's legal basis, Amy transmitted a formal proposed-closure letter using MRG.

That sequence became central to my analysis.

I interpret it as an example of a government official attempting to use **administrative policy, terminology, or internal practice as a substitute for governing law**.

That distinction is not semantic.

It goes directly to the structure of political power.

A bureaucracy can create manuals, classifications, internal terminology, workflow practices, and policy categories.

But administrative convenience is not the same thing as enacted law or duly promulgated regulation.

This repository therefore asks:

> **What happens when an individual invokes the governing law and the administrator responds through institutional policy instead?**

And more specifically:

> **Did Amy administer my case according to the legal standard governing rehabilitation, or did she use an internal policy framework to produce a different political and administrative result?**

That dispute is one of the central subjects of this archive.

## Maximum Rehabilitation Gain as the Political Fault Line

The dispute over **Maximum Rehabilitation Gain (MRG)** crystallized the larger conflict.

According to Amy's own explanation quoted in my affidavit, MRG was a closure category used when a veteran completed **some, but not all**, objectives of a rehabilitation plan.

My position was straightforward:

> **I completed all of them.**

My affidavit identifies:

- completion of my graduate program;
- compliance with the healthcare objective;
- participation in employment services;
- submission of job-search documentation;
- entrepreneurial activity;
- and receipt of Employment Adjustment Allowance payments.

I therefore argued that MRG contradicted both the factual record and Amy's own description of the classification.

But my objection went further.

I asserted that **MRG itself lacked a formal regulatory definition in the governing CFR provisions** and that Amy was attempting to substitute a policy-derived administrative category for the regulatory status I contended the law required.

In my analysis, this was not merely bad paperwork.

It represented a collision between:

- **law and policy;**
- **individual rights and bureaucratic discretion;**
- **documented completion and institutional classification;**
- **veteran autonomy and administrative control;**
- **a record of achievement and a politically consequential refusal to recognize that achievement.**

That collision is one of the principal reasons this repository exists.

From my perspective, this was the moment when the deeper political structure became unmistakable.

## The Same Institutional Pattern I Had Already Reported

My affidavit expressly compares what I experienced inside VR&E with the institutional behavior I had previously challenged as a federal whistleblower.

The comparison is not that the subject matter was identical.

The comparison concerns **method**.

I observed what I understood as a recurring pattern:

1. a governing record exists;
2. the record produces an inconvenient result;
3. officials introduce a different administrative interpretation;
4. the interpretation is treated as authoritative because the institution says so;
5. requests for the underlying legal authority remain unanswered or unresolved;
6. procedural complexity shifts attention away from the original factual question; and
7. the institution ultimately generates documentation supporting the outcome it prefers.

In the CFPB context, I had challenged what I identified as manipulation of data and examination processes.

In the VR&E context, I came to believe I was witnessing manipulation of administrative classifications, rehabilitation records, and procedural frameworks.

That comparison is my analysis.

This repository exists in part to test it against the source record.

When I describe Amy as a **political operative**, I am not limiting the phrase to campaign employment or party work.

I use it in a structural sense.

Amy exercised government power.

She administered access to a federally funded rehabilitation program.

She participated in decisions affecting education, employment, economic support, vocational classification, case closure, and the official federal record describing my rehabilitation.

Those are exercises of public authority.

A person does not become politically neutral merely because the authority is exercised through paperwork, counseling, case-management systems, or administrative procedure.

My inquiry therefore examines Amy as:

- a counselor;
- a federal employee;
- an administrator;

## Veteran Status, Class, and Political Dependency

I do not romanticize veteran status.

A government can publicly praise military service while simultaneously placing veterans into systems of profound administrative dependency.

The veteran may have served the state, yet afterward be required to repeatedly demonstrate:

- disability;
- eligibility;
- compliance;
- employability;
- rehabilitation;
- need;
- credibility;
- and adherence to institutional procedure.

That relationship is political because government officials possess the authority to determine whether the veteran receives resources necessary for economic and social stability.

It is social because veteran identity carries both honor and stigma.

It is economic because access to benefits can materially determine the veteran's ability to survive.

It is religious in the sense that American political culture can treat military service and government authority through narratives of sacrifice, loyalty, obedience, and national virtue that discourage examination of how power actually operates.

My relationship with Amy is one field site through which I study that structure.

## Administrative Language as Political Power

One of the lessons I draw from this relationship is that political power often does not announce itself as political power.

It appears as:

- a status code;
- a closure category;
- a counselor note;
- a policy manual;
- an unanswered email;
- a revised interpretation;
- an internal system entry;
- a requirement introduced after the fact;
- a distinction between "employed" and "self-employed";
- or a sentence declaring that a bureaucratic result is a "positive outcome."

That language matters because administrative words produce consequences.

A classification can determine what the government officially says happened.

This archive therefore treats language itself as evidence.
